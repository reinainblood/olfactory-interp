**Question.** When the *same molecule* is elicited under two different names, does its odor-frame-minus-printed-name state identify the partner alias better than chance? How do the odor-only states, printed-name-only states and a character spelling baseline compare? Cross-alias identification is a test of molecule-specific information in this prompt construction. Even perfect retrieval would be compatible with learned chemistry, source knowledge and semantics of synonyms; it would not establish odor sensation, valence or an exclusively olfactory mechanism.

**Eligibility.** The planned set contains 96 ordinary, unpatched, maximum-effort natural responses: two aliases × odor/name frame × 24 fit molecules. Wait for the complete four-cell panel for every included molecule. Follow the study’s existing state-acknowledgment and welfare exclusions. If fewer than two molecules remain, report unestimable. No partial, outcome-favored gallery.

**Scores fixed in advance.** At native `attn_in` layers 23, 47 and 71, take the 7,168-dimensional captured vector at the single fully tokenized neutral marker. For each alias, form three matrices indexed by molecule: O (odor frame), N (printed-name frame), and D = O − N (the same alias’s frame contrast). For each view and alias, subtract its across-eligible-molecule mean, L2-normalize rows and compute the full name-to-alias cosine matrix. Use its transpose for alias-to-name. If a centered row is numerically zero (norm ≤ 1e−12), mark that view/layer unestimable without removing competing identities to rescue it. The spelling baseline uses L2-normalized counts of contiguous 2-, 3- and 4-character n-grams of each NFKC/casefolded, whitespace-normalized chemical name, including start/end markers. Preserve punctuation, numerals and stereochemical text. It uses no fit, model readout or odor rating.

**Denominators and ties.** For each direction, query the entire opposite-alias gallery, scoring the matching CID against every eligible candidate. Repeat retrieval within the physical node’s eligible gallery when it contains at least two molecules; retain and report singleton exclusions. Name/alias directions and layers reuse the same molecules, and the nodes form technical strata. There are at most 24 molecule identities, never 48 independent queries or 144 independent cases.

**Hard-foil test.** Each query identity has one prespecified competing CID, selected from eligible molecules without using any activation. Prefer another member of the same frozen family; if none exists, choose the highest molecular fingerprint Tanimoto similarity in the frozen nuisance table. Within a family prefer highest fingerprint similarity. Resolve exact ties by increasing numeric CID. Scores compare the correct alias vs this same foil in each direction, awarding 1, 0.5 or 0. Report foil provenance, candidate reuse and the attainable 0.5 label-symmetric reference. Overlapping pairs do not support an independent-trials binomial p-value.

For the all-molecule gallery, permute freely. For the within-node gallery, permute independently within node (singleton blocks contribute no test query). Compare the observed mean top-1 credit to its label-shuffle distribution and report its mean, 95% central interval and plus-one upper-tail reference probability. This is descriptive under exchangeability of candidate labels; chemical families/aliases can remain partly predictable. It does not identify odor content. Report all three nominated layers, no best-layer selection.
