How much of a smell survives its name?

A molecule can go by two names. We used that fact to ask what Kimi retains when it considers an odor, then designed a harder test. Can a cue written inside the model change a judgment when the question gives away neither the name nor the smell?

Sunday afternoon checkpoint · Data through Sunday, November 22, 2026 at 2:00:00 PM CST

Two names for one molecule

In 96 ordinary forward passes, Kimi K3.1 Pro considered either a molecule’s odor or its printed name. Each of 24 fitting compounds appeared under two aliases. We read the same neutral marker in each prompt and subtracted its printed-name state from its odor state. Could the difference find the matching alias among 24 candidates?

The difference recovered the correct alternate name in 10, 12 and 13 of 48 searches. Spelling alone recovered 35. Using the odor frame alone matched or exceeded the difference at every layer. Both search directions reused the same 24 molecules. A chemical name can carry clues about identity and structure, and identity alone cannot establish sensory quality.

One exception is worth seeing. In a descriptive check after scoring, methyl octanoate retrieved methyl caprylate at all three layers even though the spelling search missed that direction. All three hits came from the same molecule.

Can the cue travel without the name?

A writer is a learned mapping from a profile of odor judgments to a small change in the model’s internal state. The registered design assigns 24 compounds to fitting and four to layer selection and delivery checks. The eventual behavioral comparison gives Kimi the same unnamed question and seed under an odor write, a name write, a mismatched odor and a sham.

The fitting pipeline had yet to record a writer at the cutoff. At 0.05×, 5/16 nominated delivery-check records were complete, covering all four conditions for 0/4 development compounds. A completed four-compound delivery decision was pending at this cutoff. The proposed hidden-odor intervention still needs a verified path to the downstream site.

What counts as delivery?

We check the same prompt position four layers downstream. Each of at least three of four development compounds must show an odor-direction projection at least 0.10 above the absolute projection of a mismatched odor, and greater than three times the absolute difference between two sham runs. The initial dose is 0.05× the natural scale with a 1% local norm ceiling. Passing qualifies the intervention for a behavioral test. A claim about steering still requires the independent odor-versus-name comparison on 12 held-out compounds.

Why a changed answer is only the start

Before fitting an odor writer, we changed one coordinate at layer 47 by one representable step. The coordinate was chosen for an arithmetic check. For vanillin acetate, the sampled paths shared 9 output tokens before diverging, and the final choice changed from eugenol to whiskey lactone. For allyl hexanoate, the sampled paths shared 76 output tokens before diverging, and both runs chose whiskey lactone. Three serving nodes reproduced those two prompt-and-seed cases. The six zero-edit controls replayed their clean token sequences exactly.

A tiny, arbitrary edit can change a sampled trajectory. Specificity requires the intended odor profile to move judgments in its predicted direction more reliably than a name, a wrong odor or an empty write. In a separate calibration, 12/12 explicit controls returned the expected letter and 12/12 favored it at the first natural answer position.

The longer-term question is whether a physical nose could supply a cue for recognition, recall or a household decision. These weekend measurements test a prerequisite inside a language model. Emotional memory and a sensor-to-model interface each need their own experiment.
